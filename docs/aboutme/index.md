#About Me
Austin Hill

  I have come to study mechanical engineering on a circuitous path. I have worked in assembly and finishing, as a machine operator, then as a CNC programmer. I now work as a mechanical designer. I specialize in taking a broken mechanical contrivance and reverse-engineering it for replacement in cases where it can no longer be purchased, from its original vendor, in a timely or economic fashion.  It is my personal belief that all practices are forms of art, and that all art is, in its essence, storytelling.  Every part, component, and feature tell a small piece of a story. Every decision made, either for efficacy, economy, or convenience totals together to reveal the quality of anything that is made. I see engineering as an adventure in problem solving. How to get from a, to z, with a full alphabet at the end, but we won’t get any q’s delivered until next Thursday. It is part of the story that, depending on one’s countenance, is either where you get to understand the depth and breadth of the story or, you get up to stretch your legs and head to the bathroom. My goal in seeking higher education is to be able to improve on designs rather than simply replicating what is there. This required more knowledge in physics and mechanics than I had before starting down this path. I am slowly learning exactly how much I did not know. It is exciting. 

  To defend an engineering decision is to communicate the rationale, based on scientific and engineering principles, that have led to the decision that was made. It requires thorough understanding of the physical principles that govern whatever the supposed decision is about as well as the technical knowledge of how whatever action the decision regards will be taken. I have the ability to defend an engineering decision in the shop-floor context. That context is a lot like the engineering context, only with a lot more swearing… and everybody has a blowtorch.
